# Corporate Information

Corporate information can add important context to market-data research.

A chart may show that something happened in the market.

A filing, financial statement or company announcement may help explain what information became available around the same period.

The purpose of this document is to show how corporate information can be connected to a market observation without turning timing alone into a claim of causation.

## Why Corporate Information Matters

Market-data research does not always end with the chart.

A price move, volume change or unusual sector pattern may lead to a second question:

**Was there a documented corporate event around the same time?**

Relevant sources may include:

- regulatory filings
- company announcements
- financial statements
- earnings releases
- investor-relations documents
- corporate-action notices
- amended or revised documents

These sources can add context, but they should remain separate from the market observation until the relationship has been checked.

## Observation First, Explanation Second

Suppose a chart shows an unusual move on a particular date.

The first statement should describe the market observation.

Example:

```text
Observation:
The instrument showed an unusually large move during the selected session.
```

The next step may be to check corporate information.

Example:

```text
Corporate context:
A company announcement was published during the same period.
```

Those two facts may be related.

But they do not automatically prove causation.

A stronger research note keeps the distinction visible.

## Source Hierarchy

When corporate information is used as evidence, prefer the most direct source available.

A practical order may be:

1. original regulatory filing
2. official company announcement
3. issuer investor-relations page
4. financial statement or presentation
5. secondary reporting or summary

The exact order can vary depending on the research question.

The principle is simple:

**When possible, trace the information back to the original corporate source.**

## Filing Context

A filing should be recorded with enough detail to identify the exact document being reviewed.

Useful fields may include:

```text
Company:

Document title:

Document type:

Filing or publication date:

Publication time:

Acceptance time:

Source:

Revision or amendment status:
```

Not every field will be available for every document.

If a field cannot be confirmed, record the uncertainty.

Example:

```text
Acceptance time:
Not available
```

## Publication Time vs Market Time

Corporate information and market data can use different time conventions.

A filing may show:

- publication time
- acceptance time
- processing time
- update time

A chart may show:

- trade time
- candle close time
- source update time
- observation time

Those timestamps should not be treated as interchangeable.

For example:

```text
Corporate publication time:
19 October 2026, 13:45 UTC

Chart observation time:
19 October 2026, 14:30 UTC
```

This makes the sequence easier to review.

## Amended and Revised Documents

Corporate information can change after the original publication.

Examples include:

- amended filings
- revised financial statements
- corrected announcements
- updated investor materials
- restated figures

A research note should identify whether the document being used is the original version or a later revision.

Example:

```text
Revision status:
Amended filing

Original publication date:
18 October 2026

Amendment date:
19 October 2026
```

This matters because a later document may contain information that was not available when the market observation occurred.

## Financial Statements

Financial statements can provide context for company performance, but the reported numbers should be tied to the correct reporting period and document version.

Useful context may include:

- reporting period
- publication date
- accounting period
- currency
- units
- restatement status
- source document

Example:

```text
Reporting period:
Q3 2026

Currency:
USD

Units:
Millions

Source:
Quarterly financial statement

Revision status:
No amendment identified
```

The objective is to make the figure traceable.

## Company Announcements

Company announcements may provide useful context around:

- management changes
- product launches
- corporate actions
- financing events
- operational updates
- earnings information
- strategic changes

The announcement should be recorded as a source.

The market interpretation should remain separate.

Example:

```text
Corporate source:
Official company announcement

Observation:
Announcement published before the market move

Interpretation:
Potentially relevant context

Causation:
Not established
```

This structure helps prevent timing alone from becoming proof.

## Corporate Actions

Corporate actions can directly affect historical market data.

Examples include:

- stock splits
- reverse splits
- dividends
- symbol changes
- mergers
- spin-offs
- contract or listing changes

When a historical chart changes around a corporate-action date, the research should check whether the data source applies an adjustment.

A useful note may include:

```text
Corporate action:
Stock split

Effective date:
Confirmed

Source:
Official corporate notice

Chart adjustment:
Source A: adjusted
Source B: not confirmed
```

This can help explain differences in historical series.

## Correlation Is Not Causation

A corporate event and a market move can happen close together without proving that one caused the other.

A research note should distinguish between:

**Temporal relationship**  
The two events occurred near each other in time.

**Documented relationship**  
The source explicitly links the market-relevant event to the company development.

**Interpretation**  
The researcher believes the corporate event may help explain the market observation.

Those categories are different.

Keeping them separate improves traceability.

## Corporate Information Record

A compact corporate-information record may look like this:

```text
Company:
Example issuer

Corporate source:
Official investor-relations page

Document title:
Example corporate announcement

Document type:
Company announcement

Publication date:
19 October 2026

Publication time:
13:45 UTC

Revision status:
No amendment identified

Related market observation:
Large price move during the same trading session

Temporal relationship:
Announcement preceded the observed move

Documented causation:
Not established

Possible interpretation:
The announcement may provide relevant context

Verification needed:
Review additional primary-source documentation
```

## Connecting Corporate Information to Market Data

A practical sequence is:

**Observe the market → Identify the time window → Search for relevant corporate information → Verify the original source → Record the relationship → Preserve uncertainty**

The sequence matters.

It helps prevent the explanation from shaping the observation too early.

## Regional Context

Corporate information can also vary by jurisdiction and reporting system.

For researchers working with Canadian market information, including Ontario-based workflows, relevant corporate records may come from different regulatory, issuer or market sources.

The geographic context can affect which source system is most relevant.

It should not change the verification standard.

The same questions still apply:

- Is the source primary?
- Is the publication time clear?
- Is the document current?
- Has the document been amended?
- Does the source support the interpretation being made?

## Final Principle

Corporate information should add context to a market observation, not replace it.

The chart records what happened in the market.

The corporate source records what information was published.

The research process connects those two layers carefully and preserves what still remains uncertain.

**Research. Compare. Verify.**
